## Clinical Summary: Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF)

At 23 Weeks Pregnant (Second Trimester) , you have 17 weeks remaining until your 40w0d estimated due date. Your primary ACOG clinical focus during Week 23 is: 24–28 week gestational diabetes screening (Glucola), repeat CBC + Serum Ferritin check (target >=50 ng/mL), and Tdap vaccination window.

## 3-Tier Obstetric Triage & Warning Sign Protocol (Week 23 Pregnant)

         TIER 1 &bull; PHYSIOLOGICAL NORM

### Expected Physiological Adaptation

        - Mild round ligament stretching, nasal congestion, or irregular non-painful Braxton Hicks tightening during Second Trimester.

        - Blood pressure consistently below 120/80 mmHg (and strictly under 140/90 mmHg ).

         TIER 2 &bull; SAME-WEEK MD CONSULT

### Same-Day OB-GYN Office Evaluation

        - Dysuria (burning with urination), persistent vomiting preventing oral hydration (>24 hours), or intense generalized itching on palms/soles (screen for intrahepatic cholestasis).

        - New mild blood pressure elevation ( 130–139 / 80–89 mmHg ) or calf asymmetry.

         TIER 3 &bull; IMMEDIATE ER / L&amp;D TRIAGE

### Immediate Labor & Delivery / Emergency Triage

        - Preeclampsia Red Flags: Blood pressure >=140/90 mmHg , severe headache unresponsive to acetaminophen, visual scotomata, or right upper quadrant pain.

        - Vaginal bleeding, gush/trickle of amniotic fluid, regular contractions (>=6/hr before 37w), or  =28w).

## Frequently Asked Clinical Questions

                What prenatal tests are needed at 23 weeks pregnant?
24–28 week gestational diabetes screening (Glucola), repeat CBC + Serum Ferritin check (target >=50 ng/mL), and Tdap vaccination window.
    What symptoms require immediate OB-GYN evaluation at 23 weeks?
Contact your OB-GYN or Labor & Delivery triage immediately for vaginal bleeding, leaking amniotic fluid, blood pressure >=140/90 mmHg, severe persistent headache, visual scotomata, regular contractions before 37 weeks, or decreased fetal movement (after 28 weeks).
