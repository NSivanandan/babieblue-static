Clinical Summary: Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF)

At 5 Weeks Pregnant (First Trimester), you have 35 weeks remaining until your 40w0d estimated due date. Your primary ACOG clinical focus during Week 5 is: Confirm intrauterine gestational sac & fetal cardiac activity via transvaginal ultrasound; take 400–800 mcg methylfolate/folic acid.

3-Tier Obstetric Triage & Warning Sign Protocol (Week 5 Pregnant)

TIER 1 • PHYSIOLOGICAL NORM

Expected Physiological Adaptation

  • Mild round ligament stretching, nasal congestion, or irregular non-painful Braxton Hicks tightening during First Trimester.
  • Blood pressure consistently below 120/80 mmHg (and strictly under 140/90 mmHg).
TIER 2 • SAME-WEEK MD CONSULT

Same-Day OB-GYN Office Evaluation

  • Dysuria (burning with urination), persistent vomiting preventing oral hydration (>24 hours), or intense generalized itching on palms/soles (screen for intrahepatic cholestasis).
  • New mild blood pressure elevation (130–139 / 80–89 mmHg) or calf asymmetry.
TIER 3 • IMMEDIATE ER / L&D TRIAGE

Immediate Labor & Delivery / Emergency Triage

  • Preeclampsia Red Flags: Blood pressure ≥140/90 mmHg, severe headache unresponsive to acetaminophen, visual scotomata, or right upper quadrant pain.
  • Vaginal bleeding, gush/trickle of amniotic fluid, regular contractions (≥6/hr before 37w), or <10 fetal movements in 2 hours (at ≥28w).

Frequently Asked Clinical Questions

What prenatal tests are needed at 5 weeks pregnant?

Confirm intrauterine gestational sac & fetal cardiac activity via transvaginal ultrasound; take 400–800 mcg methylfolate/folic acid.

What symptoms require immediate OB-GYN evaluation at 5 weeks?

Contact your OB-GYN or Labor & Delivery triage immediately for vaginal bleeding, leaking amniotic fluid, blood pressure >=140/90 mmHg, severe persistent headache, visual scotomata, regular contractions before 37 weeks, or decreased fetal movement (after 28 weeks).
