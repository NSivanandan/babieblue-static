Born in 1909, Dr. Virginia Apgar was an American obstetrical anesthesiologist who in 1952 invented the 'Apgar Score'—the 1-minute and 5-minute newborn vitality check (Appearance, Pulse, Grimace, Activity, Respiration) still performed on virtually every baby born around the world today. Her systematic protocol transformed modern neonatology and dramatically reduced infant mortality. Why are suffragist-era names like Eleanor, Clara, Cora, and Harriet so popular again? Because of the 100-year generational cycle! The 19th Amendment was ratified in 1920, meaning the bold women and babies of that era lived roughly a century ago. To today's parents, names from the 1890–1925 window sound vintage, literary, and grounded without feeling like the names of their own parents' generation. What is 'Narrative Identity' and how does a namesake story help a child? Psychologist Dan McAdams' research on Narrative Identity shows that children build resilience from the family stories they are told about who they are and where they came from. Knowing she was named after NASA mathematician Katherine Johnson, Red Cross founder Clara Barton, or novelist Louisa May Alcott gives a child a built-in anchor of courage and curiosity. ## Quantitative Pediatric Reference & Evaluation Matrix The table below summarizes board-reviewed pediatric benchmarks, developmental norms, and clinical action thresholds for 101 Unique Girl Names Inspired by American History: Abolitionists, Pioneers, Scientists, Literary Icons & SSA Revival Trends per AAP, WHO, and CDC guidelines.
