## Clinical Summary: Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF)

- **Primary Clinical Focus:**
    Eradicating urogenital *Trichomonas vaginalis* protozoan infection in women and pregnant patients using the updated CDC STI Treatment Guideline regimen (Metronidazole 500 mg PO twice daily for 7 days) alongside mandatory concurrent partner treatment.

- **Evidence-Based Protocol & Targets:**
    Diagnose via high-sensitivity Nucleic Acid Amplification Test (NAAT / Aptima swab, 95–100% sensitivity vs. only 50% on wet-mount microscopy), treat all symptomatic women and pregnant patients with oral Metronidazole 500 mg BID x 7 days (which halves treatment failure compared to the old 2g single dose), abstain from sex until both partners complete therapy, and retest at 3 months.

- **When to Escalate to OB-GYN or Emergency Care:**
    Contact your OB-GYN promptly if you experience frothy yellow-green vaginal discharge, vulvar itching/strawberry cervix, dysuria, or if you are pregnant and develop pelvic pressure, contractions, or watery fluid leakage (as untreated *T. vaginalis* increases Preterm Premature Rupture of Membranes [PPROM] by ~40%).

Caused by the protozoan parasite Trichomonas vaginalis, it primarily affects the urogenital tract in both men and women. Transmission typically occurs through sexual activity, including vaginal, oral, or anal intercourse with an infected partner. However, it is important to note that it can also be transmitted through other forms of contact with the genital area, such as sharing sex toys. Due to its prevalence and ease of transmission, trichomoniasis is considered to be one of the most common STIs globally. ## Understanding the Transmission of Trichomoniasis Trichomoniasis, a commonly occurring sexually transmitted infection (STI), is primarily transmitted through sexual contact. The causative agent, Trichomonas vaginalis, thrives in moist environments, such as the genital area. The transmission can occur through unprotected sexual intercourse, whether vaginal, anal, or oral. It is worth noting that the infection can still be passed on even if ejaculation does not occur. In some instances, the infection may also be transmitted through sharing contaminated sex toys or by mother-to-child during childbirth. The risk of contracting trichomoniasis is higher when engaging in sexual activities with multiple partners. This is because the infection can be spread from an infected individual to their sexual partner(s) through the exchange of bodily fluids. Additionally, individuals with a history of other sexually transmitted infections or compromised immune systems may also be more susceptible to acquiring trichomoniasis. It is crucial to understand the modes of transmission to take appropriate preventive measures and protect oneself from this common STI. ## Recognizing the Signs and Symptoms of Trichomoniasis Trichomoniasis, commonly known as trich, is a sexually transmitted infection caused by the parasite Trichomonas vaginalis. However, it is important to note that not all individuals infected with trichomoniasis show symptoms . In fact, approximately 70% of affected women and around 90% of affected men remain asymptomatic. Nevertheless, for those who do experience symptoms, it is crucial to recognize and seek medical attention promptly to prevent further complications. In women, the most common symptom of trichomoniasis is a yellow-green vaginal discharge that may have a foul odor. This discharge can be accompanied by itching, redness, and swelling in the genital area. Additionally, some women may also experience pain or discomfort during urination or sexual intercourse. It is worth noting that the symptoms in women can vary in intensity, and some may even mistake them for a yeast infection or bacterial vaginosis. Therefore, it is essential to consult a healthcare professional for a proper diagnosis and appropriate treatment. ## The Impact of Trichomoniasis on Women's Health Trichomoniasis, a common sexually transmitted infection (STI), can have a significant impact on women's health . This parasitic infection is caused by trichomonas vaginalis, a microscopic organism that spreads through sexual contact. When infected, women may experience a range of unpleasant symptoms, including itching, burning, and a frothy vaginal discharge. In addition to these physical discomforts, trichomoniasis can also cause long-term complications that can affect a woman's overall well-being. If left untreated, trichomoniasis can lead to serious health issues for women . One of the most concerning complications is an increased risk of acquiring other STIs, such as HIV. The inflammation caused by trichomoniasis can make it easier for HIV to enter the body, making women more susceptible to this life-threatening infection. Additionally, trichomoniasis has been associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes, such as preterm birth and low birth weight. It is crucial for women to seek timely diagnosis and appropriate treatment to minimize the potential impact of trichomoniasis on their health and well-being. ## Trichomoniasis in Men: Symptoms and Complications Trichomoniasis is a sexually transmitted infection (STI) caused by the parasite Trichomonas vaginalis. While it is more commonly associated with women, men can also contract trichomoniasis through sexual activity with an infected partner. However, the symptoms and complications in men may differ from those experienced by women. In men, trichomoniasis can manifest as various symptoms, although some individuals may not exhibit any signs at all. Common symptoms include an itching or burning sensation inside the penis, discomfort during urination, and white, yellow, or green discharge from the penis. It is important to note that these symptoms can resemble those of other STIs or infections, making it crucial to seek medical attention for an accurate diagnosis. If left untreated, trichomoniasis in men can lead to complications such as prostatitis (inflammation of the prostate gland) and urethritis (inflammation of the urethra). Therefore, it is vital for men to be aware of the symptoms and seek early treatment to prevent further health issues. ## Risk Factors for Contracting Trichomoniasis Trichomoniasis is a sexually transmitted infection that can affect both men and women. While anyone who is sexually active can contract trichomoniasis, certain risk factors can increase the likelihood of infection . Engaging in unprotected sexual intercourse, particularly with multiple partners, is one of the main risk factors associated with contracting trichomoniasis. The infection spreads through direct genital contact, making individuals with a higher number of sexual partners more vulnerable to infection. Another risk factor for trichomoniasis is having a history of sexually transmitted infections (STIs). Those who have previously been diagnosed with an STI, such as chlamydia or gonorrhea, are more susceptible to acquiring trichomoniasis. Additionally, certain behavioral factors, such as engaging in high-risk sexual activities like anal sex, can further increase the chances of contracting the infection. It is important to note that while trichomoniasis is more prevalent in individuals with these risk factors, anyone who is sexually active should take necessary precautions to protect against this common STI. ## Diagnosis of Trichomoniasis: Tests and Procedures Trichomoniasis, a common sexually transmitted infection , can have serious health consequences if left untreated. Therefore, it is crucial to accurately diagnose the infection in order to provide appropriate treatment. The diagnosis of trichomoniasis involves a combination of tests and procedures that help identify the presence of the Trichomonas vaginalis parasite, the causative agent of the infection. The most common diagnostic method for trichomoniasis is a laboratory test called a wet mount. During this procedure, a sample of vaginal discharge is collected and examined under a microscope to check for the presence of the parasite. In some cases, a culture test may also be performed to grow the parasite in a lab and confirm the diagnosis. Additionally, newer molecular tests, such as polymerase chain reaction (PCR), are becoming more prevalent in diagnosing trichomoniasis as they offer higher sensitivity and quicker results. These tests often involve collecting a vaginal swab or urine sample, which is then analyzed to detect the genetic material of the parasite. ## Treatment Options for Trichomoniasis Treatment options for trichomoniasis primarily involve the use of medications that can effectively eliminate the parasite causing the infection. The most commonly prescribed medication for this sexually transmitted infection (STI) is metronidazole. This antibiotic works by targeting and destroying the trichomonas parasite, helping to relieve symptoms and prevent further transmission of the infection . Metronidazole can be administered orally in the form of pills or as a topical gel for treating external symptoms. In some cases, healthcare providers may prescribe alternative medications such as tinidazole or secnidazole. These medications have a similar mechanism of action to metronidazole and are effective in eradicating the trichomonas parasite as well. It is important to follow the prescribed dosage and complete the full course of treatment to ensure the infection is completely eliminated. Additionally, it is advisable to abstain from sexual activity or use condoms during treatment to prevent reinfection or spreading the infection to others. ## Preventive Measures to Avoid Trichomoniasis Trichomoniasis is a sexually transmitted infection (STI) that can spread through sexual contact with an infected partner. Taking preventive measures is crucial to avoid contracting this STI and to reduce the risk of its transmission. One of the most effective preventive measures is practicing safe sex, which involves consistent and correct use of condoms during sexual intercourse. Condoms act as a barrier, preventing the exchange of bodily fluids that may contain the Trichomonas vaginalis parasite. Another important preventive measure is to limit the number of sexual partners and engage in mutually monogamous relationships . This reduces the risk of exposure to the infection and decreases the likelihood of transmission. Regular screening for STIs, including trichomoniasis, is crucial, especially for individuals with multiple or new sexual partners. Early detection and prompt treatment can help prevent further spread of the infection. Additionally, open and honest communication with sexual partners is essential to ensure that both individuals are aware of any potential STI risks and can take necessary precautions. By adopting these preventive measures, individuals can significantly reduce their chances of contracting and spreading trichomoniasis. ## Support and Resources for Individuals Affected by Trichomoniasis Support and resources are essential for individuals affected by trichomoniasis, as they navigate the challenges and concerns associated with this sexually transmitted infection. Because **70% to 85% of people with Trichomonas vaginalis have zero symptoms**, the organism can live quietly in the male urethra or female lower genital tract for months or even years without causing discharge or itching. Changes in vaginal pH (such as pregnancy hormones, menstruation, or a shift in vaginal lactobacilli) can cause a long-dormant colonization to suddenly multiply and show up on a sensitive NAAT swab. Why did the CDC stop recommending the '1-day (2 gram) mega-dose' of Metronidazole for women? For years, doctors gave a single 2,000 mg dose of Metronidazole for convenience. However, a landmark randomized clinical trial published in *The Lancet Infectious Diseases* proved that women taking **500 mg twice daily for 7 days** had nearly half the rate of treatment failure compared to the 1-day dose (11% vs. 19%), with fewer nausea side effects. Is it true you can't use vaginal Metronidazole gel (MetroGel) to treat Trichomoniasis? Yes—that is a critical clinical pearl! While vaginal metronidazole gel works great for Bacterial Vaginosis (BV), it **fails in more than 50% of Trichomoniasis cases** because *Trichomonas* also colonizes the urethra and deep paraurethral (Skene's) glands where vaginal gel cannot reach. Always use oral tablets. ## Quantitative Clinical Reference & Intervention Matrix The table below summarizes board-reviewed diagnostic thresholds, pharmacological safety rules, and clinical action protocols for Trichomoniasis in Pregnancy (2021–2026 CDC Guidelines): Why Metronidazole 500 mg BID x 7 Days Beat the 2g Single Dose & Partner EPT per ACOG, AAP, WHO, and IDSA guidelines.
