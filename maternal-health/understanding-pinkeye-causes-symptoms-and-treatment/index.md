## Clinical Summary: Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF)

- **Primary Clinical Focus:**
    Differentiating the 4 distinct causes of a red or goopy eye in maternal-child medicine: 1) Benign Congenital Nasolacrimal Duct Obstruction (blocked tear duct in 6–20% of newborns: yellow mucus in the corner of a WHITE eye), 2) Ophthalmia Neonatorum (true newborn conjunctivitis in the first 28 days: red swollen eyelids + pus requiring emergency gonococcal/chlamydial rule-out), 3) Pediatric Bacterial Conjunctivitis (Haemophilus influenzae / Strep pneumoniae—Look for the 'Otitis-Conjunctivitis Syndrome' ear infection!), and 4) Adenoviral / Allergic Conjunctivitis.

### Newborn, Toddler & Maternal Pinkeye Differential & Daycare Triage Tool

       1. Age Group &amp; Appearance of the Eye

         Newborn/Infant (&lt;6m): Yellow/Sticky Goop Matting Lashes, But the Eye Itself Is Crisp WHITE
         Newborn (&lt;28 Days Old): Red Eye, Puffy/Swollen Eyelids &amp; Thick Pus Discharge!
         Baby/Child: Thick Yellow-Green Pus Re-Accumulating All Day (+ Ear Tugging or Fever)
         Watery/Crusty Morning Discharge + Cold/Cough (Viral) OR Both Eyes Itchy (Allergic)

       2. Vision, Pain &amp; Contact Lens Status

         Mild Gritty Feeling, Normal Vision, No Contact Lenses
         Wears Soft Contact Lenses (Risk of Pseudomonas Corneal Ulcer!)
         Tiny Blisters on Eyelid, Severe Light Sensitivity, Blurry Vision, or Whole Eye Socket Swollen

### Amber Zone: Prompt OB-GYN or Specialist Workup

        - Thick yellow-green pus that refills in the eye within 15 minutes of wiping it away throughout the daytime (bacterial conjunctivitis—call your doctor for Polymyxin B/Trimethoprim or Erythromycin eye drops).

        - A toddler with goopy pinkeye who is also tugging at their ear or waking up crying at night (ask the pediatrician to look inside both ears for Conjunctivitis-Otitis Syndrome).

        - Any redness or discharge in a person who wears contact lenses (remove lenses immediately and see an optometrist/MD).
