## Clinical Summary: Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF)

For a 10 lb (4.54 kg) baby, the standard AAP weight-calibrated Acetaminophen (Tylenol) 160 mg/5 mL liquid dose is 1.77 mL (56.8 mg) every 4 to 6 hours as needed (never exceed 5 doses in 24 hours). Because a 10 lb infant is typically under 6 months old, Ibuprofen is contraindicated due to immature renal prostaglandin clearance; consult your pediatrician before administering any antipyretic.

## 3-Tier Pediatric Fever Triage Protocol (10 lb Infant)

         TIER 1 &bull; PHYSIOLOGICAL NORM

### Home Comfort & Oral Hydration

        - Infant is >=3 months old, alert, taking breastmilk/formula, and playful when temperature drops after 1.77 mL acetaminophen.

        - Dress in a single breathable layer; avoid cold baths or alcohol rubs which trigger shivering vasoconstriction.

         TIER 2 &bull; SAME-WEEK MD CONSULT

### Same-Day Pediatrician Evaluation

        - Infant is 2 to 3 months old with any fever >=100.4°F (call pediatrician before or immediately after dosing).

        - Fever persists >72 hours, reaches >=104.0°F repeatedly, or is accompanied by ear tugging or painful urination.

         TIER 3 &bull; IMMEDIATE ER / L&amp;D TRIAGE

### Immediate Emergency Department (911 / ER)

        - Infant is under 60 days old with a rectal temperature >=100.4°F (38.0°C) — do not mask fever prior to ER exam.

        - Inconsolable high-pitched crying, bulging fontanelle, purple non-blanching rash, grunting, or febrile seizure.

## Frequently Asked Clinical Questions

                How many mL of infant Tylenol (160 mg/5 mL) can a 10 lb baby take?
A 10 lb (4.54 kg) baby takes 1.77 mL (56.8 mg) of 160 mg/5 mL liquid acetaminophen every 4 to 6 hours as needed, up to a maximum of 5 doses in 24 hours. If your infant is under 12 weeks old, always call your pediatrician before giving Tylenol.
    Can a 10 lb baby take Motrin (Ibuprofen)?
No. Ibuprofen is only approved for infants 6 months of age and older. For a 10 lb infant under 6 months, avoid Ibuprofen and contact your pediatrician.
