## Clinical Overview & Diagnostic Criteria

- **Primary Clinical Focus:**
              Establishes a precise Estimated Due Date (EDD, 40w 0d) and current gestational age (weeks + days) using four ACOG/AIUM-endorsed dating pathways: menstrual cycle + luteal phase calibration, assisted reproductive technology (IVF Day-3 or Day-5/6 blastocyst transfer), known ovulation/IUI date, or first-trimester ultrasound biometry.

- **Evidence-Based Protocol & Mathematical Standard:**
              Standard human gestation spans 280 days (40 weeks) from the first day of the Last Menstrual Period (LMP) assuming a 28-day cycle and 14-day luteal phase, or 266 days (38 weeks) from fertilization. In IVF cycles, Day-5 blastocyst transfers add 261 days, while Day-3 cleavage-stage transfers add 263 days.

- **When to Escalate to Your OB-GYN or Specialist:**
              Per ACOG Committee Opinion No. 700, if first-trimester crown-rump length (CRL) ultrasound differs from LMP dating by more than ±5 days (prior to 9w0d) or ±7 days (between 9w0d and 13w6d), formal EDD redating by your OB-GYN is clinically indicated.

## Clinical Methodology: How Obstetric Gestational Dating Works

Human pregnancy is clinically tracked in gestational age (menstrual age) rather than embryonic age. Historically codified by German obstetrician Franz Karl Naegele, standard gestational dating assigns 280 days (40 weeks) from the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP). However, classic Naegele’s Rule relies on two rigid biological assumptions: that the patient has an exact 28-day menstrual cycle and that ovulation occurs on Cycle Day 14.

In reproductive endocrinology, follicular phase length (the interval from menstrual day 1 to ovulation) accounts for nearly all cycle-to-cycle variation, whereas the luteal phase (from ovulation to menstruation) remains relatively conserved at 12 to 15 days. Consequently, a woman with a regular 34-day cycle ovulates around Cycle Day 20—six days later than standard Naegele’s Rule assumes. Failing to calibrate for cycle length and luteal phase length creates artificial "small-for-gestational-age" alarms in the first trimester and premature post-term induction recommendations at 40 weeks.

### Why IVF Embryo Transfer Dating Is the Most Precise Anchor

When pregnancy is achieved via Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART), the exact chronological age of the embryo is known to the hour. During an in vitro fertilization cycle, fertilization Day 0 corresponds to 2 weeks 0 days (14 days) of gestational age. Therefore:

  - Day 5 Blastocyst Transfer: On the day of transfer, the patient is already clinically 2 weeks 5 days (19 days) pregnant, leaving exactly 261 days until the 40w0d EDD.

  - Day 3 Cleavage-Stage Transfer: On the day of transfer, the patient is clinically 2 weeks 3 days (17 days) pregnant, leaving 263 days until the 40w0d EDD.

Per ACOG Committee Opinion No. 700, an ART-derived due date should never be changed based on subsequent ultrasound scans, because embryo transfer chronology is more accurate than biometric ultrasound estimation.

### ACOG Sub-Classification of Term Pregnancy (Weeks 37 to 42)

Because neonatal respiratory maturity, thermoregulation, and hepatic bilirubin conjugation continue maturing rapidly between weeks 37 and 39, ACOG and SMFM replaced the broad "37-to-42-week term" label with four distinct clinical classifications:

  - Early Term (37w 0d through 38w 6d): Elective delivery without medical indication is discouraged due to higher rates of transient tachypnea of the newborn (TTN) and feeding challenges.

  - Full Term (39w 0d through 40w 6d): The gold-standard physiological window with the lowest neonatal morbidity and mortality.

  - Late Term (41w 0d through 41w 6d): Warrants enhanced fetal surveillance (non-stress testing and amniotic fluid index assessment) and discussion of labor induction.

  - Post-Term (42w 0d and beyond): Associated with uteroplacental senescence, oligohydramnios, and meconium aspiration risk; delivery is clinically recommended by 41w0d–42w0d.

## Frequently Asked Clinical Questions (Physician-Reviewed)

### Why does my doctor count pregnancy from my last period if I wasn’t pregnant yet during those first two weeks?

       Obstetricians measure Gestational Age from the first day of your Last Menstrual Period (LMP) because most spontaneous conceptions do not have a confirmed lab timestamp for ovulation. Thus, Weeks 1 and 2 represent follicular maturation leading up to ovulation around Day 14, making your Gestational Age exactly 2 weeks ahead of the embryo’s actual post-fertilization age.

### How does having a 32-day or 35-day menstrual cycle change my due date?

       A longer menstrual cycle almost always reflects a longer follicular phase (delayed ovulation) rather than a longer luteal phase. In a 35-day cycle with a normal 14-day luteal phase, ovulation occurs on Day 21 (+7 days later than a 28-day cycle). Our calculator automatically adds those +7 days to your 280-day baseline so your pregnancy is not misclassified as measuring behind on your first ultrasound.

### When will an OB-GYN change my due date after an ultrasound?

       Under ACOG Committee Opinion No. 700, an LMP-based due date is only changed if a first-trimester crown-rump length (CRL) ultrasound differs by more than ±5 days before 9w0d, or more than ±7 days between 9w0d and 13w6d. In the second trimester (16w0d–21w6d), a discrepancy greater than ±10 days is required.

### How is an IVF due date calculated for a Day 5 blastocyst vs. a Day 3 embryo?

       Full-term gestation from fertilization is 266 days. For a Day 5 blastocyst transfer, the embryo has already developed for 5 days, so we add 261 days (266 − 5) to the transfer date. For a Day 3 cleavage embryo transfer, we add 263 days (266 − 3) to the transfer date.

### What percentage of babies are actually born on their exact Estimated Due Date?

       Large obstetric registry studies demonstrate that only 4% to 5% of babies arrive on their exact 40w0d Estimated Due Date. Approximately 82% of spontaneous singleton births occur within the 3-week window between 38w0d and 41w3d.

### Does carrying twins change how my Estimated Due Date is calculated?

       The mathematical 40w0d EDD is calculated identically for twins and singletons so fetal growth charts remain standardized. However, clinical delivery occurs earlier for twins—typically around 37w0d–38w0d for dichorionic-diamniotic twins and 34w0d–36w6d for monochorionic twins.
