## Clinical Summary: Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF)

At 10 Days Past Ovulation (10 DPO / 3 weeks 3 days gestational age) , the median quantitative serum beta-hCG level for a viable singleton pregnancy is 12 mIU/mL (normal physiological range: 5–28 mIU/mL ), while twin gestations average a median of 22 mIU/mL (range: 11–53 mIU/mL ). Clinical viability is determined not by a single isolated draw, but by a 48-hour serial rise of at least 49% to 66% .

## 3-Tier Early Pregnancy Beta-hCG & Ectopic Triage Protocol (10 DPO)

         TIER 1 &bull; PHYSIOLOGICAL NORM

### Reassuring Serial Doubling Trajectory

        - 48-hour serum beta-hCG increases by >=66% (doubling every 48–72 hours when under 1,200 mIU/mL).

        - Mild midline uterine cramping without vaginal bleeding or unilateral adnexal tenderness.

         TIER 2 &bull; SAME-WEEK MD CONSULT

### 48-Hour Repeat Draw & OB-GYN Review

        - 48-hour rise is between 35% and 65% , or single hCG draw is below expected DPO range due to late ovulation.

        - Beta-hCG exceeds 2,000–3,500 mIU/mL — schedule transvaginal ultrasound to confirm intrauterine gestational sac.

## Frequently Asked Clinical Questions

                What is a normal hCG level at 10 DPO?
At 10 DPO (3w3d), the median singleton serum beta-hCG is 12 mIU/mL, with a broad physiological 10th–90th percentile range of 5 to 28 mIU/mL. Because implantation timing varies from 6 to 12 DPO, obstetricians evaluate the 48-hour doubling velocity rather than a single number.
    Does a high hCG level at 10 DPO mean twins?
While twin pregnancies have a higher median hCG at 10 DPO (22 mIU/mL vs. 12 mIU/mL for singletons), there is substantial overlap. Only a transvaginal ultrasound at 6+ weeks can definitively diagnose a twin gestation.
