## 4. 2025–2026 AAP Preemie RSV Antibody Rules (Beyfortus & Enflonsia) & Visitor Cocooning

## 3-Tier Pediatric & Neonatal Triage Warning Protocol

Use this color-coded clinical triage grid to distinguish expected newborn/infant physiology from symptoms requiring same-day pediatric clinic evaluation or immediate Pediatric Emergency Department care.

         TIER 1 • ROUTINE HOME CARE

### Green Zone: Expected Physiology & Routine Care

        - Steady home weight gain of 20–30 grams/day on prescribed breastmilk fortification or 22 kcal/oz transitional formula.

        - Reaching rolling, smiling, and babbling milestones right on time when plotted against Corrected Age (due date age).

        - Maintaining 6+ heavy wet diapers per 24 hours and finishing oral feeds within 20–25 minutes without fatigue.

         TIER 2 • CALL PEDIATRICIAN (24H)

### Amber Zone: Call Pediatrician Within 24 Hours

        - Feeds consistently taking >30 minutes with sweating, milk dribbling, or falling asleep before finishing half the bottle.

        - Inguinal bulge in the groin that appears when crying and flattens when calm (reducible inguinal hernia—common in preemies; schedule surgical consult).

        - Mild gastroesophageal reflux or straining with iron drops (never stop preemie iron without checking ferritin/reticulocytes).

         TIER 3 • IMMEDIATE PEDIATRIC ER / 911

### Red Zone: Immediate Pediatric ER or 911 Evaluation

             When do pediatricians stop adjusting for prematurity?

Most pediatricians and CDC milestone trackers adjust for prematurity until 24 months (2 years) of corrected age, and up to 36 months for extreme micro-preemies born before 28 weeks' gestation.

 Do I still adjust milestones for a "late-preterm" baby born at 35 or 36 weeks?

Yes! At 35 weeks' gestation, a baby's brain weighs only ~65% of a 40-week full-term newborn's brain. Subtracting 4 to 5 weeks prevents unnecessary anxiety when tracking the 2-month social smile, 4-month wake windows ( infant wake windows and nap transition schedule ), and 6-month sitting readiness.

 Why do NICU graduates grunt, squeak, and strain loudly all night once they get home?

Known affectionately as "preemie grunting," this is usually benign Infant Dyschezia (learning to relax the pelvic floor while pushing gas/soft stool—see newborn stool color and texture clinical triage chart ) combined with active REM sleep. Additionally, NICU babies are accustomed to 50–55 dB of ambient incubator fan and monitor hum; a dead-silent bedroom startles them awake. Use a continuous white noise machine set strictly ≤50 dB placed at least 7 feet from the crib.
