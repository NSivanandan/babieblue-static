## Clinical Summary: Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF)

- **Primary Clinical Focus:**
    Preventing accidental under-dispensing (caloric deprivation / hyponatremia) or over-dispensing (hypernatremic dehydration) in automated formula machines via kitchen gram-scale verification and strict 4-bottle funnel cleaning.

- **Evidence-Based Protocol & Targets:**
    Verify exact brand-and-stage powder setting code at every can change; clean and bone-dry the mixing funnel after every 4th bottle so caked powder never blocks the dosing wheel.

- **When to Escalate to Pediatrician or Emergency Care:**
    Seek immediate pediatric evaluation if your infant exhibits poor weight gain, lethargy, sunken fontanelles, or excessive thirst while using an automated dispenser—switch to hand-scooped bottles immediately.

## Frequently Asked Clinical Questions (Pediatrician Reviewed)

     Why does a 4 oz bottle made by a Baby Brezza look like 4.5 oz in the bottle?

When you mix 4 fl oz of water with 2 scoops (~17.2 to 18 grams) of formula powder, the dissolved powder displaces liquid volume, raising the final meniscus to roughly 4.4–4.5 fl oz. Hand-scooping 2 scoops into 4 fl oz of water produces the exact same final volume.

     How do I test if my Baby Brezza is dispensing the right amount of formula powder?

Perform the official plastic-wrap test: line the inside of the clean, dry funnel with plastic wrap (leaving the water hole open), run a 4 fl oz cycle, and weigh the captured dry powder on a kitchen gram scale. Compare that weight to 2 scoops (or 4 scoops for EU 1:1 formulas) listed on the nutrition label.

     Does the Baby Brezza sterilize powdered formula against Cronobacter?

No. Its warmest setting is approximately 104°F (40°C), which is ideal for immediate drinking without destroying probiotics, but well below the 158°F (70°C) threshold required to kill Cronobacter sakazakii in powdered formula.

## 3-Tier Pediatric & Neonatal Triage Warning Protocol

Use this color-coded clinical triage grid to distinguish expected newborn/infant physiology from symptoms requiring same-day pediatric clinic evaluation or immediate Pediatric Emergency Department care.

         TIER 1 • ROUTINE HOME CARE

### Green Zone: Expected Physiology & Routine Care

        - Dry powder gram test matches the formula can's scoop weight times number of scoops.

        - Two funnels rotated so a 100% bone-dry funnel is installed after every 4th bottle.

        - Distilled or cooled boiled water used in the reservoir and changed every 24–48 hours.

         TIER 2 • CALL PEDIATRICIAN (24H)

### Amber Zone: Call Pediatrician Within 24 Hours

        - Visible damp powder ring caked on the underside of the powder container hole (causes under-dosing).

        - Baby suddenly waking every 60–90 minutes hungry or showing flattened weight velocity on machine-made bottles.

        - Cloudy biofilm or mineral scale visible in the water tank (run 1:1 distilled white vinegar descale cycle immediately).

         TIER 3 • IMMEDIATE PEDIATRIC ER / 911

### Red Zone: Immediate Pediatric ER or 911 Evaluation

        - Weight loss, sunken fontanelle, lethargy, or fewer than 4 wet diapers in 24 hours (rule out machine under-dispensing).

        - Fever ≥100.4°F (38.0°C), projectile vomiting, or abnormal neurological movements in an infant under 60 days.

        - Mold growth discovered inside the internal silicone tubing or water reservoir.
